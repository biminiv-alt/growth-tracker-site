Average Height for an 18-Year-Old Boy

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

At 18, most boys are at or very close to their adult height. Here is the full CDC percentile table and what a late developer can still expect.

The short answer

The average (50th percentile) height for an 18-year-old boy in the U.S. is about 5 ft 9.4 in (176.2 cm), based on CDC growth charts. About half of 18-year-old boys are taller and half are shorter. Anything from about the 3rd to the 97th percentile is in the normal range.

Is he still growing?

Most boys finish growing between about 16 and 18. Boys who started puberty late can keep growing a little longer, usually adding an inch or less. Signs he may have a bit left: his voice and facial hair came late, or he grew noticeably in the last 6 months. See how much a boy can grow after 17.

How his height compares with his family

By 18, family genes explain most of where a boy ends up. If his adult height is well below what you would expect from both parents, it is worth a conversation with his doctor. See how accurate the family-height formula is.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average height for an 18-year-old boy?

About 5 ft 9.4 in (176.2 cm), the 50th percentile on the CDC growth chart.

What height is tall for an 18-year-old boy?

About 6 ft 0.9 in (185.3 cm) is the 90th percentile and about 6 ft 1.9 in (187.8 cm) is the 95th.

Can an 18-year-old boy still grow?

Most boys have reached or nearly reached adult height by 18. Late developers can still add a little, usually an inch or less, until their growth plates close.

How can I tell if he is still growing at 18?

Measure him the same way every 3 to 6 months. If he has gained less than about a quarter inch over 6 months, he is almost certainly at his adult height.
