How to Talk to Your Son About Puberty

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Most boys would rather not have the big talk. Here is how to cover puberty in small, low-pressure pieces, and how to handle the height questions that often come with it.

The short answer

Start around 9 or 10, before the changes begin, and keep it to short, casual talks. Many small conversations work better than one big one. Let him know he can ask anything, and that everyone goes through puberty at a different time.

What to cover

  • Growth spurts: some boys grow early, others late, and both are normal.
  • Hygiene: showers, deodorant and clean clothes. See body odor in boys.
  • Body hair and voice: what to expect and roughly when.
  • Feelings: moods can swing, and that is part of it. See mood swings in puberty.
  • Sleep and food: he will need more of both.

Easy ways to start

MomentOpener
In the car"Some guys in your grade are probably starting to change. Anything you have wondered about?"
Buying deodorant"This is a good time to start using this every day."
After a checkup"The doctor said you are right on track. Want to see your growth chart?"

When height is the worry

Boys who start late often feel left behind. It helps to explain that late starters usually catch up, and to show him his own numbers. See when he is upset about being short and when boys start puberty.

What to say at each age

AgeGood topics
8 to 9Bodies change at different times, and that is normal
10 to 11Growth spurts, body hair, sweat and deodorant, voice changes
12 to 14Wet dreams, skin care, sleep, why friends may be taller or shorter
15 and upQuestions he raises, plus checking in on how he feels about his size

Short talks often work better than one big one. Car rides and side-by-side activities make it easier for many boys. If he is shorter than his friends, the most useful message is that timing varies a lot and late starters often catch up. See when he is upset about being short and when boys catch up to girls.

Frequently asked questions

When should I talk to my son about puberty?

Start around 9 or 10, before most changes begin, so nothing comes as a surprise.

How do I start the conversation?

Short, casual moments work best, like in the car or while doing something together. A book or a news story can be an easy way in.

What should I cover?

Growth spurts, body hair, body odor and hygiene, voice changes, mood swings, and that everyone starts at a different time.

What if he is worried about being shorter than his friends?

Explain that boys start puberty at different ages, and late starters often catch up. Showing him his family-height range can help.
