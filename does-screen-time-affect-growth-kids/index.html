Does Screen Time Affect a Child's Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Video games and phones will not shrink him. But they can crowd out two things that matter for growth: sleep and moving his body. Here is what to focus on.

The short answer

Screen time does not directly stunt growth. His height is mostly set by family genes, nutrition and overall health. Where screens can matter is indirectly: late-night use can cut into sleep, and long hours sitting can replace active play. Protect those two and screens are not a growth concern.

Why sleep matters

The body releases much of its growth hormone during deep sleep, especially early in the night. Bright screens and stimulating games late in the evening can make it harder to fall asleep. Most kids aged 6 to 12 need about 9 to 12 hours, and teens need about 8 to 10.

Simple rules that help

  • Screens off about an hour before bed
  • Phones and tablets charge outside the bedroom overnight
  • No screens during meals, so he eats a full meal
  • Some active play, sport or time outside most days

Screens and activity

Hours on screens are often hours not spent moving. Regular activity supports healthy weight, bone strength and better sleep. It does not add inches beyond his genes, but it helps him reach them. See sports where size matters less.

Frequently asked questions

Does screen time stunt a child's growth?

There is no good evidence that screens directly reduce height. Genes and overall health set most of a child's growth.

How can screens affect growth indirectly?

Late-night screen use can shorten and disturb sleep, and long hours on screens can replace active play. Good sleep and regular activity both support healthy growth.

How much sleep do kids need?

Children 6 to 12 need about 9 to 12 hours a night, and teens 13 to 18 need about 8 to 10 hours.

What is a simple screen rule that helps sleep?

Screens off about an hour before bed and phones charging outside the bedroom overnight.
