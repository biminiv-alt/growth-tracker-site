About The Growth Path

The Growth Path is a resource for parents who are quietly worried about their son's height, growth, or puberty timing, and want a clear read on when to bring the concern to a pediatrician.

What this site is

Educational content. Nothing on this site is medical advice, and nothing on this site is a diagnosis. Everything here is written to help you have a better conversation with your son's pediatrician, not to replace one.

What this site is not

We do not sell supplements. We do not sell height-increase products. We do not sell fear. The category is full of both, and we chose the opposite lane on purpose. If you ever see an ad or affiliate link on this site pointing to a growth-in-a-bottle product, please report it and we will kill it.

Editorial process

Every article on this site goes through the same review:

  1. Draft against the source list below. If a claim is not supported by CDC, AAP, NIH, or a peer-reviewed pediatric endocrinology reference, we do not publish it.
  2. Cross-check numbers. Percentile ranges, velocity benchmarks, and the mid-parental height formula come from CDC 2000 Growth Charts and standard pediatric texts, not from popular articles.
  3. Voice check. We rewrite for parents, not clinicians. If a paragraph would only make sense to a doctor, we rewrite it.
  4. Fear check. Any sentence that would make a worried parent more anxious without giving them a next step gets rewritten or cut.

Our source list

These are the sources we use, in this order of preference:

  • CDC 2000 Growth Charts: percentile curves and growth velocity benchmarks.
  • American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org): parent-facing guidance on growth, puberty, and when to seek care.
  • NIH-NICHD: Growth: research-backed overviews of pediatric growth conditions.
  • MedlinePlus Growth Charts: consumer-safe explanations of clinical measurements.
  • AAP Clinical Guidelines: for clinical thresholds and referral criteria.
  • Peer-reviewed pediatric endocrinology literature (via PubMed), used sparingly for specific claims.

Any factual claim you read here can be traced back to one of the above. If you find something that cannot, please tell us and we will fix it.

Reviewer profile

The Growth Path is written and reviewed against pediatric endocrinology sources by a small editorial team. We do not claim any single named clinician as author of individual pages; each page is a synthesis of the sources above. Where a specific pediatric endocrinologist reviews content, we say so on that page.

When to see a pediatrician

If you are here because your son's growth feels off, the single most useful thing we can tell you is: bring a log to his next visit. Six months of measurements plus his family-height context is often enough for a pediatrician to say "this is fine" or "let's look closer." Your visit is more useful with data than without.

If you want a starting point: our Growth Check pulls his current percentile against CDC data and his family-height target range in 60 seconds. Free, no account.
