How Do You Know if Growth Plates Are Closed?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Once growth plates close, height stops. Here are the signs you can see at home, and how doctors check for certain.

The short answer

Only an X-ray can tell you for sure. At home, the best sign is that he has grown less than half an inch over a full year, measured the same way, along with finished puberty: an adult voice, regular shaving and a body that has filled out.

How doctors check

A bone age X-ray of the hand and wrist shows how open the growth plates are and can estimate how much growth is left. It is quick, uses a very small dose and is ordered by his doctor.

Why it matters

If his plates are still open, there is still growth to come, and treatments for a medical growth problem can still help. Once they close, height is final. More in when growth plates close in boys.

Frequently asked questions

How can you tell if growth plates are closed without an X-ray?

You cannot be certain, but strong signs are growth under half an inch over a full year, an adult voice, regular shaving and shoe size that has stopped changing.

At what age do boys' growth plates close?

Most boys' growth plates close between about 14 and 19, most often around 16 to 17. Late bloomers close later.

What X-ray shows if growth plates are closed?

Usually an X-ray of the left hand and wrist, called a bone age X-ray. A knee X-ray can also show the plates in the leg bones.

Can you grow after growth plates close?

Not in a meaningful way. Height can vary a little through the day and with posture, but the long bones stop lengthening.
