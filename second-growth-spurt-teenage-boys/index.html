Can a Teenage Boy Have a Second Growth Spurt?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Not in the way most people mean. Boys typically have one main puberty growth spurt. What looks like a second spurt is usually the late-puberty tail or a delayed developer catching up.

The short answer

Boys have one main puberty growth spurt, typically peaking between 13 and 15. What looks like a "second spurt" is one of three things:

  1. The late-puberty tail. Growth continues at declining rates for 1-3 years after peak. A brief acceleration in the tail can feel like a new spurt.
  2. Delayed development. A boy who has not yet had his real spurt at 15-16 will have it later, and it can feel out of sync with peers.
  3. Medical treatment. Boys with undiagnosed thyroid, celiac, or growth hormone issues who get treated may show catch-up growth that resembles a new spurt.

The one-spurt biology

Puberty triggers a cascade of testosterone and growth hormone that opens the growth plates for accelerated bone growth. Once the plates start closing (typically ages 16-18), that acceleration ends. There is no biological mechanism for a fresh independent spurt after growth plates close.

The late-puberty tail explained

After peak height velocity, growth does not stop abruptly. Typical arc:

  • Peak year: 3-4 inches.
  • Year 2: 2-3 inches.
  • Year 3: 1-2 inches.
  • Year 4: 0-1 inch.

Within this tail, a boy might grow 0.5 inches in one month and 1.5 inches over the next four. That variability inside the arc is not a second spurt, it is just the shape of the decline.

Constitutional delay: the "second" spurt that is really the first

Boys with constitutional delay of growth and puberty (CDGP) start puberty later than peers. If your son had a small spurt at 12 (sometimes called a "pre-pubertal growth spurt") and then a much bigger acceleration at 15-16, that second one is his real puberty spurt. What looked like the first spurt was just normal childhood growth.

What is NOT a second spurt

  • Weight gain during teen years. Muscle and fat mass keep changing after height stabilizes.
  • Small growth pushes at 18-20. Most boys are done by 17-18. Growth after 20 is rare.
  • Perceived "growth" from posture improvement or shoe changes.

When a late acceleration is worth investigating

  • Boys under 18 who had inadequate nutrition or chronic illness during their expected spurt years.
  • Boys treated for hypothyroidism or growth hormone deficiency in mid-teens, who show catch-up growth after treatment.
