My 16-Year-Old Son Is Still Growing. How Much Growth Could He Have Left?

Most 16-year-old boys still have 1 to 3 inches of growth ahead. Late developers can have more. Bone age is how you get a real number.

The realistic ranges

By 16, most boys are on the back half of their growth curve. How much remains depends heavily on where he is in puberty.

  • Early developer (puberty started around age 10-11, spurt peaked at 13-14): often just 0-2 inches remaining. Growth mostly done.
  • Typical developer (puberty started 11-13, spurt peaked at 14-15): 1-3 inches remaining, spread over 12-24 months.
  • Late developer (puberty started 13-14, spurt peaked at 15-16 or later): 2-5 inches remaining, spread over 2-3 years.
  • Very late developer / constitutional delay (puberty still building at 16): can have 4-6+ inches ahead. This is uncommon but real.

The signals of "still growing meaningfully"

  • Voice has changed within the last 12-18 months (rather than years ago).
  • He still needs new pants or shoes every 6 months.
  • Growth velocity is above 1 inch per year.
  • His current height is well below his mid-parental height target.
  • Bone age is delayed vs. chronological age (requires a wrist x-ray).

Signals of "close to done"

  • Voice changed 2+ years ago.
  • He has been the same height for 6+ months.
  • His growth velocity in the last 12 months is under 0.5 inches.
  • Bone age matches chronological age.

How to get a real number

Bone age imaging is the single most useful test. A single wrist x-ray shows how mature his skeleton is. Standardized tables (Greulich-Pyle) convert that to a bone age, and pediatric endocrinologists use bone age to estimate remaining growth. If bone age is 15, he probably has 2+ inches ahead. If bone age is 17, he is mostly done.

Ask his pediatrician about a bone age x-ray at his next visit. It is low-radiation, one image, and gives the clearest answer you can get without waiting for time to pass.

What NOT to spend money on

  • Over-the-counter "growth boosters." Nothing sold without a prescription has been shown to change adult height.
  • Growth hormone therapy for a healthy late-developer. GH is FDA-approved for specific diagnoses; being a late bloomer is not one of them.
  • Supplements marketed for teen height. Save the money.

What actually helps

  • Adequate sleep (8-10 hours). Growth hormone releases during deep sleep.
  • Adequate nutrition, especially protein and calories.
  • Treating chronic conditions that affect growth (thyroid, celiac).
  • Not smoking. Smoking during the growth years is associated with reduced adult height.
