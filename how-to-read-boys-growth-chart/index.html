How to Read Your Son's Growth Chart

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

The growth chart from his checkup is simpler than it looks. Here is how to read it, how to plot his height yourself, and the few patterns worth a question.

The short answer

Each curved line on the chart is a percentile. If his dots follow near the 25th line, about 25 out of 100 boys his age are shorter. What matters most is not which line he is on, but whether he keeps following his own line over time.

How to read one measurement

  1. Find his age along the bottom.
  2. Find his height up the side.
  3. Where the two meet is his dot. The nearest curved line is his percentile.

The major lines are usually the 5th, 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th and 95th. Anything from about the 3rd to the 97th is in the normal range. See what a height percentile means.

One wrong measurement can make the chart look alarming. Measure without shoes, against a flat wall, at the same time of day. See how to measure height at home.

Frequently asked questions

What do the curved lines on a growth chart mean?

Each line is a percentile. The 50th line is the middle: half of boys his age are taller and half shorter. The 10th line means 10 out of 100 boys are shorter.

Which growth chart is used for boys in the U.S.?

Doctors in the U.S. usually use the WHO charts for children under 2 and the CDC charts from 2 to 20.

What growth chart pattern is a concern?

Crossing two or more major percentile lines, especially downward, or a growth rate that slows below what is normal for his age.

Is it normal for a boy to change percentiles?

Small shifts are normal, especially in the first two years and around puberty, when early and late developers move up or down for a while.
