Track and thrive.

The other articles are one-time reads. This one is a habit. Track his growth over time. See the curve unfold. Watch the shape, not just the number.

The cadence that catches changes.

  • Every 3 monthsFrequent enough to catch a slowdown or a spurt. Not so frequent it becomes an anxious ritual.
  • Same time of dayPeople are taller in the morning than in the evening. Consistency reduces measurement noise.
  • Same wall, no shoesPick a wall, pencil the mark, log the number. Boring is good.
  • Chart itPlotting shows the shape. A steady line and a falling line are two different conversations.

What to watch for.

Not a single low number. The slope. If his line is tracking his line, that is one story. If his line is bending downward, that is another. The bend is the flag.

Height moves slowly. Anxiety does not have to.The point of tracking is not to worry more often. It is to worry less between measurements, because you know when the next data point is coming.

Long arc.

The parents who come out the other side describe it the same way: the years feel long and the arc feels short. The 2am spiral is worst at the beginning. It gets quieter as the data comes in, as the pediatrician appointments happen, as he grows into who he is regardless of the number.

"The tracking is the reason I stopped spiraling."

TK, mom of a 12-year-old
