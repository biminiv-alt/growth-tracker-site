Build his confidence.

You cannot make him taller today. You can build the confidence that carries him regardless of how tall he ends up.

Age changes everything.

The questions at 8 are different from 12, which are different from 16. At 8, he is noticing. At 12, he is comparing constantly, and locker rooms make it worse. At 16, he is deciding what kind of man he wants to be, and height is one input, not the whole story.

What lands as reassuring at 8 sounds patronizing at 14. The words have to match the age.

Three anchors for the hard conversations.

  • Do not minimize what he raisesIf he brings up his height, do not brush past it. Reflect it back. "Sounds like today was hard." Then wait.
  • Talk about what his body can do, not what it measuresSpeed, coordination, endurance. Real things. Not "you are just as tall on the inside."
  • Name the person, not the sizeHis teammates. His teachers. His friends. Every relationship he has is built on who he is, and that continues to be true no matter what the tape measure says.

The week he sits the bench.

Some coaches will bench him for size, especially in contact sports. It hurts him and it hurts you. What helps: name it directly ("I saw you did not get much playing time today, that is hard"), do not rush to fix it, do not pull him from the sport for him, and do not badmouth the coach in front of him. Ask what he wants to do next week. Follow his lead.

Confidence is the compounding asset that outlasts height.

"He is 5'6 and he is the kid other kids follow. Height was never the whole story."
