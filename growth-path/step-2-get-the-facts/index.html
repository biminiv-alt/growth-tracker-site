How to read your son's percentile.

The number your pediatrician says at every visit lands like a verdict. It is not. It is a spot on a curve. Here is what each number actually means.

  • 50th percentile Median. Half of boys his age are taller, half are shorter. The middle of the pack, nothing more.
  • 25th percentile Below-average, not "short." Roughly 1 in 4 boys is here or below. Most finish healthy and unremarkable.
  • 10th percentile Shorter side of normal. Worth watching, worth measuring on schedule, usually not a diagnosis.
  • 5th percentile and below Where a real diagnostic conversation with a pediatric endocrinologist starts. Not the end of the world. The beginning of a workup.

Growth curves: the shape matters more than the number.

This is the piece most pediatricians will not slow down to explain, and it changes everything.

A boy who has been tracking the 25th percentile his whole life is one story. His curve is steady. His slope is his slope. He is short-ish, he is on his own line, and that is usually fine.

A boy who was 50th percentile at age 4 and has dropped to 25th at age 9 is a different story. His slope changed. That is called crossing percentiles, and it is the thing a careful pediatrician is watching for. Both boys need attention. They need different kinds of attention, and Step 4 (the medical workup) moves up in urgency for the second boy.

The question is not "how tall is he today." The question is "is his curve doing what his curve was doing." Sudden drops across percentile lines are the flag. Steady low is a different conversation than falling low.

Why predictions have wide ranges (and why that is honest).

The free predictor will give you a range, not a single number. Here is why.

The best-validated adult height model in pediatrics is Khamis-Roche. Even a well-done Khamis-Roche prediction has a confidence interval of roughly plus or minus 4 inches. That means "your son is predicted to finish 5'8''" really reads "somewhere between about 5'4'' and 6'0''." That is not the tool being weak. That is biology being biology.

Anyone selling you a single-number prediction with false precision is either not doing the math or not telling you the truth. A wide range is the honest answer.

What the range actually gives you. A floor and a ceiling. The floor is almost always taller than the number you have been picturing at 2am. That alone is worth 60 seconds.
