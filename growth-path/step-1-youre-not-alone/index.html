You're not alone.

It's 2am. Your son is asleep down the hall. Your phone is face down for the fifth time and you keep picking it up. You already know the search history: "how tall will my son be," "boys below 25th percentile," "late bloomer signs," "growth hormone for short kids."

You have been carrying this for a while. Maybe since his pediatrician said "he is in the 10th percentile" like it was a weather report. Maybe since the birthday party where every other boy in the class was half a head taller. Maybe since the game he sat out because the coach said he was "still developing." You cried in the car on the way home. He didn't see. You made sure he didn't see.

Here is what nobody in your life will actually say to you: you are not overreacting. Your kid's body is one of the most primal things you are wired to notice, and worrying about it is not neurotic, it is being his mother. The people telling you "he'll be fine, my brother was short and turned out great" are trying to be kind. They are also not answering the question you actually asked, which is "what if he is not fine, and what do I do about it."

You are not the only mom Googling this at 2am. Real numbers below. And a plan, so the worry has somewhere to go.

The numbers you should know.

  • 1 in 4 Boys are below the 25th percentile for their age. Being on the shorter side is common. It also is not nothing.
  • Over 100,000 Moms Google "will my son be short" every month in the US. You are not the only one up.
  • Most Short boys are just short and finish healthy. Some are late bloomers who catch up. A smaller share have a medical cause a pediatric endocrinologist can help with, and catching it earlier is better.
  • 2 to 4 inches Documented added adult height from growth hormone therapy in kids with a clear indication (like CDGP or ISS below defined thresholds). Not a cosmetic promise. A real medical pathway your pediatric endocrinologist evaluates.
