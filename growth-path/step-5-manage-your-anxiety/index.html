Manage your anxiety.

The 2am spiral. The comparison at the school pickup. The tightness when he asks "am I ever going to be tall?" This is the parent-facing part.

Why the anxiety compounds.

The loop is predictable. You watch him at practice. You compare him to the taller kid. You picture the next ten years in about forty seconds. Then you project that fear back onto him, in your tone, in the questions you ask, in the way your hand tightens on his shoulder.

He feels it. He does not need to know what you were thinking. Kids read the room, and the room is you.

Three tools that actually help.

  • Name the trigger, not the childWhen you catch yourself comparing him to the taller kid, name the comparison silently. "I am doing the comparison thing again." That short pause is often enough to interrupt the loop.
  • Separate his data from your fearHis growth chart is a data question. Your fear is a separate problem. Handle them separately.
  • Get the visit on the calendarConcrete action is the best antidote to swirling worry. Book the pediatrician appointment. The waiting is worse than the appointment.

What to say when your partner minimizes it.

The most common version: "he'll be fine, my brother was short and turned out great." It is not dismissive on purpose. It is a different processing style. Try: "I hear you. What I need from you right now is not reassurance. I need help thinking through the pediatrician question, so I stop carrying this alone."

You cannot help him thrive if you are the most anxious person in the room.

"I was the problem I kept trying to fix in him."
