Teenage Son Moody and Irritable? What Is Normal During Puberty

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Snapping one minute and fine the next is a familiar part of puberty. Here is what is driving it, the simple things that help, and the signs that mean it is more than a phase.

The short answer

Some moodiness is a normal part of puberty. Hormones are changing, the part of the brain that manages impulses is still developing, his body clock is shifting later so he is often short on sleep, and a growing body gets hungry fast. Short bursts of irritability that pass are common. Moods that stay low or change how he lives day to day are different and worth acting on.

What usually helps

  • Sleep: teens need about 8 to 10 hours. Phones out of the bedroom at night helps more than most rules. See why teen boys sleep so much.
  • Food: regular meals and a real snack after school. Hunger makes a lot of moods worse. See why he is always hungry.
  • Activity: sport, the gym or just getting outside most days.
  • Low-pressure talk: car rides and side-by-side time often work better than a face-to-face talk.
  • Pick your moments: wait until things are calm to talk about what happened.

Signs it is more than a phase

  • Low mood, irritability or hopelessness most days for two weeks or more
  • Pulling away from friends, family or things he used to enjoy
  • Grades dropping, skipping school, or big changes in sleep or eating
  • Signs of alcohol or drug use
  • Any talk of self-harm or not wanting to be here

If you see these, talk with his pediatrician, who can screen for depression and anxiety and help you find support. In the U.S. you can call or text 988 at any time to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. If he is in immediate danger, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Are mood swings normal in teenage boys?

Yes. Changing hormones, a brain that is still developing, short sleep and hunger all make moods swing more in puberty. Short bad moods that pass are common.

How much sleep does a teenage boy need?

Teens aged 13 to 18 need about 8 to 10 hours a night. Their body clock shifts later in puberty, so many get far less on school nights.

When are a teen's mood changes a concern?

When low mood, irritability or withdrawal lasts most days for two weeks or more, or comes with falling grades, dropping friends or activities, big changes in sleep or eating, or any talk of self-harm.

Where can I get help right away?

In the U.S. you can call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline at any time. If he is in immediate danger, call 911.
