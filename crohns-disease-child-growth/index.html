Can Crohn's Disease Slow a Child's Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Crohn's disease is one of the medical conditions that can slow a child's growth, sometimes before anyone notices stomach trouble. Here is what to watch for.

The short answer

Yes. Crohn's disease can slow growth and delay puberty, and in some children slow growth shows up before obvious stomach symptoms. It is not common, but it is one of the conditions doctors think about when a child's growth slows without a clear reason. Treating the inflammation early gives him the best chance to catch up.

Signs to watch for

  • Slow growth or dropping percentiles
  • Puberty starting late
  • Belly pain, especially after eating, or frequent diarrhea
  • Weight loss or poor appetite
  • Tiredness, mouth sores, unexplained fevers or anemia

How it slows growth

Inflammation in the gut interferes with the body's growth signals, the damaged gut absorbs food less well, and children with belly pain often eat less. Some medicines used to treat it can also affect growth, which his care team will weigh.

Celiac disease is another gut condition that can slow growth. See celiac disease and growth and blood tests for short stature.

Questions to ask his doctor

  • Could slow growth be a sign of a gut problem, even without much stomach pain?
  • Which blood and stool tests would help rule it out?
  • If it is Crohn's, how will treatment protect his growth?
  • How often should we measure his height and weight?

Growth after diagnosis

When Crohn's is found and brought under control before puberty ends, many children show catch-up growth. Treatment plans for children often aim to limit long courses of steroids, which can slow growth, and may include nutrition therapy. Keep a record of his heights so his team can see the trend. Frequently asked questions

Can Crohn's disease affect a child's height?

Yes. Inflammation, poor absorption of food and eating less can all slow growth and delay puberty. Slow growth is sometimes the first sign.

What are the signs of Crohn's disease in kids?

Belly pain, diarrhea, weight loss, tiredness, mouth sores, unexplained fevers, anemia, delayed puberty and slow growth.

How is Crohn's disease diagnosed in children?

Usually with blood tests, a stool test for inflammation, and then an endoscopy and colonoscopy with a pediatric gastroenterologist.

Can a child catch up on growth after treatment?

Often yes, especially when treatment starts before his growth plates close. That is why early diagnosis matters.
