What "average" means here

The average (50th percentile) height for a 17-year-old boy in the U.S. is 175.9 cm (5' 9.3"). That means about half of 17-year-old boys are taller than this, and half are shorter. It does not mean anything is wrong with a 17-year-old who is taller or shorter, every percentile from about the 3rd to the 97th falls within the range of typical variation.

Why boys the same age can be very different heights

At 17, most boys are within an inch or two of their final adult height. A small minority, usually late developers, still have significant growth ahead. Family height is now the dominant predictor.

The variance at 17 is mostly genetics catching up. Early developers stopped growing a year or two ago. Late developers may still be climbing. Both end up in the range predicted by family height.

When to talk to a pediatrician

Height alone is rarely diagnostic. What pediatricians look for at age 17:

  • If growth has stopped and adult height is significantly below the mid-parental target (more than 2 inches under), it's worth a pediatrician review to confirm growth plates have fully closed.
  • Height below the 3rd percentile with no family explanation warrants investigation.

If any of these apply, that is a conversation to bring to his next visit, ideally with 6-12 months of measurements in hand.

Frequently asked questions

Is my 17-year-old short?

5' 5" (165 cm) at age 17 is around the 10-15th percentile. Growth is likely mostly done, final adult height probably within an inch of this.

What if my 17-year-old is tall?

6' 3" (191 cm) at age 17 is around the 97th percentile, tall, and likely at or very close to his final adult height.

Do boys still grow after 17?

A small percentage of 17-year-old boys still have 1-2 inches of growth left, especially late developers. Most are done or nearly done.

How much should a 17-year-old grow in a year?

0-1 inch per year for most 17-year-olds. Growth is essentially complete for many by this age.
