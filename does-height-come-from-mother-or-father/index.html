Does Height Come From the Mother or the Father?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

It is a common belief that boys get their height from their mother's side. Here is what is actually known.

The short answer

Height comes from both parents. Hundreds of genes play a part, and a boy inherits about half his genes from each parent. There is no good evidence that the mother's side matters more for boys, even though that idea is widely repeated.

Where the mother's-side idea comes from

Boys get their one X chromosome from their mother, and a few height-related genes sit on it. But most height genes are spread across all the other chromosomes, which come from both parents equally. The result is that both sides of the family count.

Frequently asked questions

Does a boy get his height from his mother?

From both parents. Height is shaped by hundreds of genes, inherited from mother and father, and there is no good evidence that the mother's side counts more.

How much of height is genetic?

Most of it. Studies of families and twins suggest around 80 percent of the differences in height between people come from genes, with nutrition and health making up the rest.

Can a boy be taller than both parents?

Yes. Many boys end up taller than their father, especially when nutrition and health are better than in the parents' childhood.

How do doctors estimate height from parents?

With the mid-parental height: the average of both parents' heights, plus about 2.5 inches for a boy. Most boys end within about 4 inches of that.
