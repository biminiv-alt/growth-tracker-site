Do Asthma Inhalers Stunt a Child's Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

If your son uses a daily steroid inhaler, you may have read that it can affect his height. The effect is real but small, and uncontrolled asthma can slow growth too. Here is how to weigh it.

The short answer

Daily steroid inhalers can have a small effect on height, and the benefit usually outweighs it. A large long-term study that followed children with asthma into adulthood found that those who took a daily inhaled steroid ended up about 1.2 cm, roughly half an inch, shorter on average. Most of the slowdown happened in the first year or two, and it did not keep adding up over time.

Why doctors still recommend them

  • Asthma that is not well controlled can slow growth on its own, through poor sleep, illness and missed activity.
  • Asthma attacks can be serious and sometimes need steroid pills, which affect growth more than inhalers do.
  • The height effect is small compared with the normal range between boys.

Which medicines matter

MedicineEffect on growth
Quick-relief inhaler (for example albuterol)Not a steroid, no known effect
Daily inhaled steroidSmall, mostly early in treatment, depends on dose
Steroid nasal sprayUsually minimal at normal doses
Repeated courses of steroid pillsLarger. Worth talking about long-term control.

What to do

  • Do not stop or cut back on his own. Talk to his doctor first.
  • Ask whether he is on the lowest dose that keeps his asthma controlled.
  • Make sure he uses a spacer if prescribed and rinses his mouth after. Good technique means less medicine is needed.
  • Measure his height every 6 months and bring the numbers. See what to track if his growth slows.

Frequently asked questions

Do steroid inhalers affect a child's height?

They can have a small effect. A large long-term study found children who took a daily inhaled steroid ended up about 1.2 cm, roughly half an inch, shorter as adults on average. The slowdown happened mostly in the first year or two of use and did not keep adding up.

Should my son stop his inhaler to protect his height?

No, not without talking to his doctor. Poorly controlled asthma can itself slow growth, and asthma attacks are dangerous. Doctors aim for the lowest dose that keeps asthma under control.

Do rescue inhalers affect growth?

Quick-relief inhalers like albuterol are not steroids and are not linked to reduced height.

What about steroid pills?

Repeated or long courses of oral steroids have a larger effect on growth than inhaled steroids. If he needs them often, ask his doctor about better long-term control.
