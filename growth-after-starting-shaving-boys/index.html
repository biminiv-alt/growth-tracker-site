How Much Do Boys Grow After They Start Shaving?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Starting to shave is a mid-to-late puberty signal. Most boys have 1 to 3 more inches of growth after they need to shave regularly. Late developers can have more.

The short answer

Starting to shave regularly happens later in puberty than the growth spurt itself. Most boys need to shave 12-24 months after their peak height velocity, which means the biggest growth is behind them by the time facial hair thickens. Typical remaining growth:

  • Early developer (started shaving at 14-15): 0-2 inches ahead.
  • Typical developer (started shaving at 15-16): 1-3 inches ahead.
  • Late developer (started shaving at 17+): 2-4 inches ahead, sometimes more.

Why shaving is a late-puberty signal

Signals of "more growth ahead"

  • He is still gaining an inch or more per year.
  • His voice has changed within the last 12-18 months.
  • He still needs bigger shoes or pants every few months.
  • His current height is below his family target.
  • Bone age is delayed vs. chronological age.

Signals of "close to done"

  • Voice changed 2+ years ago.
  • Growth velocity under 0.5 inches per year.
  • Same shoe size for 12+ months.
  • Full beard development already underway.

How to get a definitive answer

Bone age imaging (a single wrist x-ray) is the most accurate tool. It shows how mature his skeleton is compared to standardized references, and pediatric endocrinologists use it to estimate remaining growth. Ask his pediatrician if you want a real number instead of a range.

What supports the remaining growth

  • Adequate sleep (growth hormone releases during deep sleep).
  • Adequate calories and protein.
  • Not smoking or vaping.
  • Treating chronic conditions affecting growth.
