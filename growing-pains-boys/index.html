Growing Pains in Boys: What's Normal and When to Worry

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Your son wakes up with aching legs, and by breakfast he is fine. That pattern is usually growing pains. Here is how to tell normal aches from something that needs a doctor.

The short answer

Growing pains are common and harmless. They are aches in both legs, usually in the evening or at night, that are gone by morning. They do not cause a limp, swelling or redness, and they do not stop him playing the next day.

The usual pattern

  • Both legs, not one.
  • In the muscles, often the front of the thighs, the calves or behind the knees, not in the joints.
  • Late afternoon, evening or night, sometimes waking him up.
  • Gone by morning, with normal walking and running the next day.
  • Often after an active day of sport or running around.

What helps

  • Gentle massage of the sore muscles.
  • Light stretching of the calves and thighs, especially before bed.
  • A warm bath or a heating pad on a low setting.
  • A children's pain reliever at the right dose for his age and weight if the ache is keeping him up. Ask his pediatrician or pharmacist if you are unsure.

When it is not growing pains

Call his doctor if any of these are true:

  • The pain is in one leg only, or always in the same spot.
  • The pain is in a joint, like the knee or ankle, or the joint is swollen, red or warm.
  • He is limping, or the pain is there in the morning or during the day.
  • It comes with fever, a rash, unusual tiredness, poor appetite or weight loss.
  • It follows an injury, or it is getting worse over time.

Teen athletes can also get pain where tendons attach near growth plates, such as just below the kneecap or at the back of the heel. This tends to come with activity rather than at night, and is worth a check with his doctor.

Growing pains and the growth spurt

Achy legs do not mean a growth spurt is starting, and plenty of boys go through their spurt with no pain at all. Better signs are bigger feet, a huge appetite and early puberty changes. See signs a growth spurt is coming.

Frequently asked questions

What do growing pains feel like?

Usually a dull ache or cramp in both legs, most often in the front of the thighs, the calves or behind the knees. They come in the late afternoon, evening or night and are gone by morning.

What age do growing pains happen?

They are most common in younger children, roughly ages 3 to 12, though some older kids get them too.

Are growing pains caused by growing?

Not exactly. Despite the name, doctors do not think bone growth itself causes the pain. Tired muscles after an active day are a likely factor.

What helps growing pains?

Gentle massage, stretching, a warm bath or heating pad, and a dose of a children's pain reliever at the right dose for his age and weight can all help.

When are leg pains not growing pains?

See a doctor if the pain is in one leg only, is in a joint, is there in the morning, causes a limp, or comes with swelling, redness, fever, a rash, tiredness or weight loss.
