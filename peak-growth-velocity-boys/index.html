When Do Boys Reach Their Peak Growth Velocity?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Peak height velocity in boys typically happens between ages 13 and 15. The year of peak growth adds 3 to 4+ inches. Here is how to tell where he is in the arc.

The short answer

Most boys hit peak height velocity (PHV) between ages 13 and 15, roughly 2 years after puberty begins. The peak year typically adds 3-4 inches, and some late-developing boys hit 4-5 inches. After the peak, growth decelerates over 2-3 years.

  • Early developer: PHV around age 11-13.
  • Typical developer: PHV around age 13-15.
  • Late developer: PHV around age 15-17.

The full growth arc for boys

  • Pre-puberty (ages 6-10): 2 to 2.5 inches per year, steady.
  • Early puberty (usually 11-13): Velocity begins to rise. 2.5-3 inches per year common.
  • Peak height velocity year: 3-4 inches. This is the year most boys grow the most in their lives.
  • Post-peak decline (1-2 years after peak): 2-3 inches per year.
  • Late puberty tail (2-3 years after peak): 1-2 inches per year.
  • Final year(s): Under 1 inch per year, then growth plates close.

How to tell if he is at or near peak

  • He suddenly needs new pants and shoes every 3-4 months.
  • His voice is actively changing.
  • He grew 1+ inch in the last 3 months (measure to confirm).
  • He is eating noticeably more.
  • He is sleeping more or seems tired more often.

How to calculate his velocity

  1. Measure him today. Same conditions each time.
  2. Pull his height from 12 months ago.
  3. Subtract. That is his annual velocity.
  4. Compare against age-appropriate ranges. During peak, expect 3+ inches.

What affects peak velocity

  • Puberty timing. Early developers usually have their peak year at a younger age but at the same magnitude.
  • Genetics. Boys from taller families often have slightly higher peak velocities.
  • Nutrition during the spurt. Undernutrition can shorten the peak or lower its magnitude.
  • Sleep. Growth hormone releases during deep sleep. Poor sleep during the spurt reduces gain.
  • Chronic illness. Untreated thyroid, celiac, or GH issues can flatten the peak.

When a "small" peak year is worth checking

  • Peak year under 2.5 inches for a boy in mid-puberty.
  • Percentile dropping while growth appears to be at peak.
  • No clear spurt at all by 15-16.
  • Bone age significantly ahead of chronological age (growth plates closing early).
