Does Sugar Stunt Growth in Kids?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Sugar gets blamed for a lot. Here is what is actually known about sugar and a child's height, and what is worth watching instead.

The short answer

No, sugar itself is not known to stunt growth. What matters for height is that he gets enough protein, calories, calcium and vitamin D, and enough sleep. A diet full of sweets and soda can crowd those out.

Where sugar can matter

IssueWhy it matters for growth
Soda in place of milkLess calcium, vitamin D and protein
Snacks in place of mealsFewer nutrients overall, especially in a picky eater
Caffeinated sodas and energy drinksCan cut into sleep, when much of growth hormone is released

A simple target

The American Heart Association suggests kids aged 2 to 18 keep added sugar under about 25 grams a day. One can of regular soda is usually more than that on its own.

What helps instead

  • Three meals with protein, plus snacks during growth spurts. See nutrition for growing boys.
  • Milk or water with meals. See does milk help boys grow taller.
  • Caffeine kept to the morning, if at all. See does caffeine stunt growth.

Frequently asked questions

Does sugar stop kids from growing?

There is no good evidence that sugar itself stunts growth. A diet heavy in sweets can leave less room for protein, calcium and other nutrients that growth does need.

How much sugar should a teenage boy have?

The American Heart Association suggests children and teens aged 2 to 18 keep added sugar under about 25 grams, or 6 teaspoons, a day.

Do soda and energy drinks affect growth?

They are not known to stunt growth directly, but they often replace milk and water, add caffeine that can cut into sleep, and harm teeth.
