Can Snoring or Sleep Apnea Affect a Child's Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Loud snoring most nights is not just noise. In some children it is a sign of sleep apnea, which has been linked to slower growth. Here is what to look for.

The short answer

Snoring loudly most nights can be a sign of obstructive sleep apnea, and sleep apnea has been linked to slower growth in children. The most common cause in kids is enlarged tonsils and adenoids. Many children grow better after it is treated, so it is worth mentioning to his doctor.

Signs to watch for

  • Loud snoring three or more nights a week
  • Pauses in breathing, gasping or snorting during sleep
  • Breathing through his mouth, day or night
  • Restless sleep or unusual sleeping positions
  • Bedwetting after he had been dry
  • Daytime tiredness, irritability or trouble focusing

Why it can affect growth

Broken sleep can cut into the deep sleep when much growth hormone is released, and the extra effort of breathing at night uses energy. Some children with sleep apnea also eat less well. Growth often picks up after treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Can sleep apnea affect a child's growth?

Yes. Obstructive sleep apnea has been linked to slower growth in some children, and many show catch-up growth after it is treated.

What causes sleep apnea in children?

Most often enlarged tonsils and adenoids that block the airway during sleep. Extra weight, allergies and some face or jaw shapes also raise the risk.

What are the signs of sleep apnea in a child?

Loud snoring most nights, pauses in breathing, gasping or snorting, mouth breathing, restless sleep, bedwetting, and daytime tiredness, irritability or trouble focusing.

How is sleep apnea diagnosed in kids?

His doctor will ask about his sleep and check his tonsils. An overnight sleep study is the main test.
