Do Boys' Feet Grow Before They Get Taller?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Feet and hands often reach adult size before the rest of the body catches up. If his shoe size has jumped, a height spurt may be around the corner.

The short answer

Yes. In boys, growth roughly follows a "distal-first" pattern: feet and hands grow first, then the long bones (arms, legs), then the torso. Shoe size jumping 1-3 sizes in under a year is a common early signal of an upcoming height spurt.

Why it happens

Growth plates in the feet close before growth plates in the long bones. Feet finish growing earlier in puberty. Pediatricians often use "shoe size stability" as one signal that the height spurt is winding down.

The typical sequence

  1. Shoe size increases (usually 11-13).
  2. Hands and arms lengthen.
  3. Peak height velocity: the main height spurt (usually 13-15).
  4. Torso lengthens and shoulders broaden.
  5. Shoe size stabilizes. Height gain slows.

What a shoe-size jump predicts

  • Two or more sizes in under a year suggests the spurt is imminent or underway.
  • Shoe size stable for 12+ months in a boy past puberty usually means most height growth is done.
  • Shoe size out of proportion to height is normal for 6-18 months. It looks awkward. It resolves.

What it does not predict

Big feet do not predict tall adult height on their own. Family height (mid-parental height) is a much better predictor. Feet just tell you where in the timeline he is, not where he will end up.
