Growth charts and reference data

CDC Growth Charts

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (US)

The standard growth charts pediatricians use in the United States for children ages 2 to 20. Includes stature-for-age and weight-for-age percentiles.

cdc.gov/growthcharts

WHO Child Growth Standards

World Health Organization

Reference growth curves for children from birth to age 5. Often used alongside CDC data for younger kids.

who.int/tools/child-growth-standards

Pediatric and endocrine guidance

American Academy of Pediatrics: HealthyChildren.org

American Academy of Pediatrics

Parent-facing library from the AAP. Search for growth, puberty, and adolescence topics.

healthychildren.org

Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guidelines

Endocrine Society

The clinical guidelines pediatric endocrinologists reference for short stature, growth hormone use, and pubertal timing. Dense but authoritative.

endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines

Pediatric Endocrine Society

Pediatric Endocrine Society (PES)

Professional society for pediatric endocrinologists. Patient resources and a "find a provider" search.

pedsendo.org/patient-resources

Family and patient advocacy

MAGIC Foundation

MAGIC Foundation for Children's Growth

Long-running nonprofit for families of children with growth-related concerns. Free educational materials, family support, and an annual convention.

magicfoundation.org

Human Growth Foundation

Human Growth Foundation (HGF)

Nonprofit supporting research and family education on growth disorders. Fact sheets on growth hormone deficiency, idiopathic short stature, and related conditions.

hgfound.org

General reference

MedlinePlus: Growth Disorders

US National Library of Medicine

Neutral, well-sourced overviews with links to primary sources.

medlineplus.gov/growthdisorders

What is not here. Supplement companies, height-increase programs, and any product that guarantees inches. If it promises a specific outcome, be careful. Real medicine talks in ranges and probabilities.
