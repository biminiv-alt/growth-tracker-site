Pediatrician appointment prep

Signed in as . Print a copy to bring with you.

Why prep matters. A pediatric well-child visit is short. Coming in with organized information and specific questions helps your doctor use that time on your actual concerns, not on catching up.

What to bring

  • Your Growth Record (height entries over time). Print or have it on your phone.
  • Height of both biological parents. If either is not available, estimate.
  • Any prior growth chart printouts if you have them.
  • A short list of the top 3 questions you want answered.
  • Notes on any symptoms: fatigue, headaches, vision changes, appetite shifts, sleep concerns.

Questions to ask, general checkup

  • Where is he on the growth chart today, and where was he a year ago?
  • What is his current growth velocity in cm per year?
  • Is his weight-for-height in the expected range?
  • Are there any signs of puberty starting, and what stage would you say he is at?
  • Is there anything about his exam today you want us to keep an eye on?

Questions to ask, if you have growth concerns

  • Given his velocity and his mid-parental target, is his growth on the trajectory you would expect?
  • Has he dropped percentiles across visits?
  • Do you think a bone age X-ray would give us useful information right now?
  • Are there labs that would help rule out common medical causes of slower growth?
  • At what point would you consider a referral to a pediatric endocrinologist?

Data to bring

Height trend. List of dates and measurements. Even 3 to 4 data points over 12 months is useful.

Family heights. Both biological parents. Grandparents help too if known.

Puberty milestones. Any visible changes and roughly when they started.

Medical history. Chronic illnesses, medications, digestive issues, sleep patterns.

Framing to avoid

Asking, "Will growth hormone make him taller?" tends to shut down the conversation. It puts the doctor into risk-management mode before they have looked at the data.

Better framing: "We are tracking his growth carefully. If his velocity slows or he drops percentiles, at what point would you want to look into it further?" That invites partnership instead of a yes-or-no on a specific treatment.

After the visit

  • Write down what the pediatrician said, in his or her own words if possible.
  • Ask for a copy of the growth chart printout.
  • Add today's measurement to your Growth Record.
  • Note any follow-up (labs to schedule, next visit timing).

This page is a general prep guide, not medical advice. Your pediatrician knows your child.
