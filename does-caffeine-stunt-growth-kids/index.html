Does Caffeine Stunt Growth in Kids and Teens?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

The idea that coffee stunts growth has been around for generations. Here is what the evidence actually says, and the caffeine problem that does matter for a growing boy.

The short answer

No good evidence shows that caffeine stunts growth. Studies have not found that kids who drink coffee or soda end up shorter as adults. Height is mostly set by genes, puberty timing, nutrition and overall health.

That does not mean caffeine is harmless for a growing boy. The real issue is sleep.

The problem that does matter: sleep

Growth hormone is released mostly during deep sleep, and teens need about 8 to 10 hours a night. Caffeine stays in the body for hours, so an afternoon soda or energy drink can push bedtime later and make sleep lighter.

Short sleep on its own will not shrink a healthy boy's adult height, but good sleep supports growth, mood and school. More in sleep and height in teenage boys.

How much caffeine is OK by age

AgePediatric guidance
Under 12No caffeine is recommended
12 to 18No more than about 100 mg a day
Any age under 18Energy drinks are not recommended

How much caffeine is in common drinks

  • Brewed coffee, 8 oz: about 95 mg, and coffee-shop sizes can be two or three times that.
  • Cola, 12 oz can: about 30 to 40 mg.
  • Black or iced tea, 8 oz: about 25 to 50 mg.
  • Energy drinks: often 80 to 300 mg a can, so one can may already be over a teen's daily limit.

What to do

  • Keep caffeine to the morning if he has it at all.
  • Swap energy drinks for water or milk, which also helps him get the calcium he needs. See does milk help boys grow taller.
  • If he is tired all the time, look at his sleep schedule before reaching for more caffeine.

Frequently asked questions

Does coffee stunt a child's growth?

There is no good scientific evidence that coffee or caffeine stunts growth or lowers adult height. The bigger concern is that caffeine can cut into sleep, and growing kids need a lot of it.

How much caffeine is safe for a teenager?

The American Academy of Pediatrics advises against caffeine for children under 12 and suggests teens aged 12 to 18 keep it to no more than about 100 mg a day, roughly one cup of brewed coffee.

Are energy drinks bad for growing boys?

Pediatric groups say energy drinks are not appropriate for children or teens. Many contain a lot of caffeine and sugar, and some have more caffeine in one can than a teen should have in a day.

Where did the idea that coffee stunts growth come from?

It likely grew out of older worries that caffeine weakens bones by affecting calcium. Later research has not shown that normal caffeine intake stunts growth in kids who eat a balanced diet.
