How Often Should You Measure Your Child's Height?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Measuring too often can cause worry over normal ups and downs. Here is a simple schedule that gives you useful numbers.

The short answer

Every 3 to 6 months at home is plenty. Measure the same way, at the same time of day, and judge his growth speed over 6 to 12 months rather than from one visit to the next.

A simple schedule

WhenWhat to do
Every 3 to 6 monthsMeasure at home and write down the date and height
Every yearCheck-up height from his doctor
After 12 months of numbersWork out inches per year and compare with normal

Why not more often

  • Children often grow more in some months than others.
  • Height changes through the day by up to about half an inch. See morning vs night height.
  • Small errors in measuring add up over short gaps.

What to look for

Before puberty, about 2 inches a year or more is typical. Under about 1.5 inches a year, or a drop across percentile lines, is worth showing his doctor. See normal growth speed for boys and how to measure accurately.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I measure my child's height at home?

Every 3 to 6 months is plenty. Growth comes in bursts, so weekly or monthly numbers jump around and can cause needless worry.

What is a normal amount for a boy to grow in a year?

Before puberty, most boys grow about 2 to 2.5 inches a year. In the fastest year of puberty, 3 to 4 inches is common.

What time of day should I measure height?

The same time each time, ideally the morning. Children can be up to about half an inch shorter in the evening.
