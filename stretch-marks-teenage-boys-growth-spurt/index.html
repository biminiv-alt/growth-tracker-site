Stretch Marks on Teenage Boys: Normal During a Growth Spurt?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Many parents are surprised to find stretch marks on their son, often across his lower back. Here is why fast-growing boys get them, what to expect, and when to mention them to his pediatrician.

The short answer

Yes, it is normal. Stretch marks are common in teenage boys, especially during the fastest year of growth. They often run across the lower back, and can also show up on the thighs, hips, knees, shoulders or upper arms.

Why fast growth causes them

During a growth spurt a boy can add 3 to 4 inches in a year. His skin has to stretch to keep up, and the deeper layer can tear slightly. The marks are the visible result. Rapid weight gain and building muscle quickly can cause them too.

What to expect

  • New marks look red, pink or purple and may itch a little.
  • Over months to years they fade to pale or silvery lines.
  • They rarely disappear completely, but most become hard to notice.
  • Creams and oils have limited evidence. Moisturizing can ease itching but will not prevent or erase them.

When to mention them to his pediatrician

  • Wide, dark purple marks along with fast weight gain, a rounder face, or high blood pressure
  • Marks that appeared after using steroid creams or steroid medicines
  • Marks along with very fast growth well beyond the family pattern

These are uncommon, but worth a check.

Helping him feel OK about them

Some boys are self-conscious, especially in locker rooms or at the pool. It can help to tell him how common they are and that they will fade. See supporting a son who is self-conscious about his body.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for teenage boys to get stretch marks?

Yes. Stretch marks are common in teenage boys, especially during the fastest year of growth. They often appear across the lower back, and on the thighs, hips, knees or shoulders.

Why do boys get stretch marks on their back?

When a boy grows quickly, his skin stretches faster than it can keep up, and the deeper layer tears slightly. The lower back is a common spot in tall, fast-growing boys.

Do stretch marks go away?

They usually fade over months to years, from red or purple to pale and silvery. They rarely disappear completely.

When should stretch marks be checked by a doctor?

Mention them to his pediatrician if they are wide and dark purple along with rapid weight gain, a rounder face or high blood pressure, or if they started after using steroid creams or medicines.
