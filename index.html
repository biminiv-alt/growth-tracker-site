Questions moms actually ask

Is my son just a late bloomer?
Probably, yes. Constitutional delay of growth and puberty is the most common cause of a boy tracking below his peers, and it is usually harmless. But "probably" is not "definitely." The signs that suggest late bloomer: dad or mom hit puberty late, growth velocity is steady (not slowing), no other symptoms. The signs that suggest something else: growth has flattened, he is slipping below his own curve, or puberty has not started by age 14. The app walks you through both sides.
When should I see a pediatrician?
Any time you are worried. That is the whole answer. If you want thresholds: below the 3rd percentile, growth velocity under 5cm per year past age 4, dropping across two percentile lines, or no puberty signs by age 14 in boys. Bring a printed growth chart. Ask specifically about a bone age X-ray and a referral to a pediatric endocrinologist. If your pediatrician tells you to "wait and see" and you are not comforted, get a second opinion.
Are growth hormones safe?
For kids with a diagnosed condition (growth hormone deficiency, Turner syndrome, chronic kidney disease, ISS below certain thresholds), the safety data over 40 years is well established. Side effects exist and matter. The decision is not trivial. It involves daily injections for years, five-figure annual costs, and a family conversation about what "tall enough" means. A pediatric endocrinologist is the only person qualified to walk you through it. The app has a full article on what to ask before saying yes.
Does puberty timing affect final height?
Yes, in both directions. Boys who enter puberty late often end up taller than their mid-childhood percentile suggested, because they get extra pre-puberty growth years. Boys who enter puberty early can end up shorter than expected, because the growth plates fuse sooner. This is why a doctor asks about Tanner stage before predicting height. It is also why "he is just short now" does not tell you what he will be at 20.
How accurate are height predictions?
The mid-parental formula (dad + mom + 5 inches, divided by 2, for boys) has a confidence range of about plus or minus 4 inches. That is not a rounding error. It is a real biological range. Khamis-Roche, which uses current age, height, weight, and mid-parental, is more accurate (about plus or minus 2 inches) once your son is past age 4. Any calculator that gives you a single number to the quarter-inch is lying to you.
What if my son is in the 5th percentile?
Fifth percentile means 95 out of 100 boys his age are taller. It is not a diagnosis, but it is a flag worth watching. What matters more than the number: is he tracking (staying on his own curve) or falling (crossing lines downward). A boy who has been at the 5th his whole life often stays there and finishes fine. A boy who dropped from the 40th to the 5th over two years needs a doctor's visit. The app graphs both patterns so you can see which one you are looking at.
