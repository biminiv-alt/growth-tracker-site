What Is a Normal Growth Velocity for Boys?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Growth velocity is inches (or centimeters) grown per year, and it is the single cleanest signal about whether a boy's growth is on track. Here are the age-by-age ranges pediatricians use.

Why growth velocity matters more than percentile

Percentile tells you where a boy sits against the population today. Velocity tells you what his growth is doing right now. Percentile can drift downward for months before anyone flags it. Velocity responds within months.

If you had to pick one number to track over the years, this is it.

Age-by-age growth velocity ranges for boys

  • Ages 2-4: 3 to 3.5 inches per year. Fastest growth outside puberty.
  • Ages 4-6: 2.5 to 3 inches per year.
  • Ages 6-10 (pre-puberty): 2 to 2.5 inches per year. Very steady. Deviations here are the most common velocity flag.
  • Ages 10-13: 2 to 3 inches per year. Early puberty pushes higher; still-pre-puberty stays lower.
  • Peak height velocity (usually 13-15): 3 to 4 inches per year. Some hit 4+ during peak year.
  • Late puberty tail (15-17): 1 to 2 inches per year.
  • Near adult height (16-18): Under 1 inch per year is normal.

How to calculate at home

  1. Measure today. Same time of day, no shoes, back flat against a wall, book flat on his head.
  2. Pull his height from 12 months ago. Most pediatric portals show dated data.
  3. Subtract. That is his velocity for the year.

When a slow year is worth raising

  • Under 2 inches per year for a pre-puberty boy age 6-12.
  • Under 1.5 inches per year during what should be the puberty spurt (13-15).
  • Slowing plus percentile crossing downward across 6-12 months.
  • Over 14 with slow growth AND no signs of puberty.

What the pediatrician does with the number

  1. Physical exam and Tanner staging.
  2. Multi-year growth chart review.
  3. Bone age x-ray (single wrist image).
  4. Basic labs (thyroid, IGF-1).
  5. Referral to pediatric endocrinology if red flags surface.
