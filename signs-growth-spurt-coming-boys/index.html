Signs a Growth Spurt Is Coming in Boys (and How Soon)

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Most boys give you clues months before the inches show up. Here are the signs, the order they usually arrive in, and roughly how long you have to wait once you see them.

The short answer

A boy's growth spurt does not arrive out of nowhere. It sits in the middle of puberty, and puberty sends signals first. The most reliable early clues are bigger feet, a much bigger appetite, body odor and the first pubic hair. Once those show up, the fastest year of height gain usually follows within about 1 to 2 years.

For most boys the peak comes between 13 and 15. For late bloomers it can be 15 or 16, and that is still normal.

8 signs a growth spurt is on the way

  1. His feet grow first. Hands and feet speed up before the long bones of the legs and spine. If he has gone up two shoe sizes in a year, height often follows within months. More on this in why boys' feet grow before their height.
  2. He is always hungry. A sudden jump in appetite, second dinners and a raided pantry are classic. His body is fueling the growth that is about to happen.
  3. He fills out a little first. Many boys put on some weight before they stretch up. It is common, and it usually evens out once height catches up. See weight gain before a growth spurt.
  4. Body odor and oily skin. Adrenal hormones kick in around the same time as early puberty. New deodorant needs and the first pimples are early signals.
  5. The first pubic and underarm hair. Pubic hair is usually one of the first visible signs of true puberty. Underarm hair tends to come a bit later.
  6. He sleeps more and later. Teens need 8 to 10 hours, and puberty shifts the body clock later. Long weekend sleep-ins are common during this stretch.
  7. Clumsiness and aches. When the feet and legs grow faster than his coordination, he may trip more or knock things over. Some boys get dull leg aches at night. Ask the pediatrician about pain that is sharp, in one spot, or limits activity.
  8. His voice starts to crack. Voice change usually overlaps with the fastest growth rather than coming well before it. If his voice is already deepening, he is likely in or near the peak. See how much boys grow after their voice changes.

The whole timeline can be shifted earlier or later by two or three years, and both ends are normal. What matters most is that the steps happen in order and that he keeps growing at a steady pace in the meantime.

How to know it has started

The only real proof is the tape measure. Measure him every three months, same time of day, shoes off, back to the wall. Before puberty, boys grow about 2 to 2.5 inches a year. If the pace of the last few months works out to 3 inches a year or more, the spurt is underway. Our guide to measuring accurately at home walks through it.

When to ask the pediatrician

  • No signs of puberty at all by his 14th birthday.
  • Puberty signs before age 9.
  • He is growing less than 2 inches a year and dropping across percentile lines.
  • Puberty started but stalled for a year or more.

None of these mean something is wrong. They are simply the points where a closer look, sometimes a bone age x-ray, is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first sign of a growth spurt in boys?

The first physical sign of puberty in boys is testicle growth, which parents rarely see. The first signs most moms notice are bigger feet, a much bigger appetite and body odor. The height spurt usually follows about a year or more later.

How long after puberty starts do boys have a growth spurt?

The fastest growth usually comes about 1.5 to 2 years after the first signs of puberty, most often between ages 13 and 15. Late developers hit it later, sometimes 15 or 16.

How much do boys grow during a growth spurt?

At the peak, many boys grow 3 to 4 inches in a single year. Across the whole of puberty, boys typically add around 10 to 14 inches.

Do boys sleep more before a growth spurt?

Many do. Teens need 8 to 10 hours of sleep, and the shift to later sleep times plus the demands of puberty often show up as long weekend sleep-ins. Extra sleep is a common companion of puberty rather than a precise predictor.
