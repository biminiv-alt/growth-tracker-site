How tall will my son be?

By The Growth Path · September 21, 2026 · 6 min read

You are staring at his growth chart or measuring the doorframe again, wondering if there is any way to know what he ends up at. There is. It's not a guess. Here is the formula pediatricians use, along with a way to run it in about 30 seconds.

The short answer

The single most reliable predictor of your son's adult height that doesn't require a doctor's office is the mid-parental height formula. It uses both parents' heights to estimate the range where his adult height will most likely land.

Boys: (father's height + mother's height + 5 inches) ÷ 2 = target adult height, plus or minus 4 inches for the likely range.

Example: dad is 5'10" (70 inches), mom is 5'5" (65 inches). Target = (70 + 65 + 5) ÷ 2 = 70 inches = 5'10". Likely range: 5'6" to 6'2".

Why this formula works

About 80% of adult height is determined by genetics. The rest is nutrition, sleep, health, and hormonal timing. The mid-parental height formula captures the biggest lever (parents' genetics) and gives you a plus-or-minus range that captures roughly 95% of outcomes.

For girls, the formula flips: (dad's height + mom's height - 5) ÷ 2.

Where it can go wrong

The formula assumes normal biological parenting on both sides. It gets less accurate when:

  • Extreme parental heights (very tall or very short parents). Regression toward the mean is stronger than the formula captures.
  • A child has an undiagnosed medical condition affecting growth.
  • A child has an early or delayed puberty pattern that changes when growth stops.
  • You are using step-parent heights instead of biological parent heights.

For the vast majority of families, it gets within 2 inches of the actual adult height.

How to use it well

Run the formula. Note the target and the range. Now check your son's current CDC percentile (see our height percentiles table).

Three cases:

Case 1: Current percentile matches the family prediction

He's on track. If the formula predicts 5'8" and he's on the 40th percentile now, that's exactly where the model says he ends up. Nothing to do except keep an eye on velocity at annual checkups.

Case 2: Current percentile is LOWER than family prediction

He might be a late bloomer, or something is limiting his growth. Look at growth velocity. If he's growing at 2+ inches per year and dad remembers being short until high school, that's a strong late-bloomer signal. If growth velocity has slowed, ask your pediatrician.

Case 3: Current percentile is HIGHER than family prediction

Rare but possible. Usually means he entered puberty early and is going to finish sooner than average. His final height often lands closer to the family prediction than his current percentile suggests.

A more precise formula (Khamis-Roche)

The mid-parental formula is 80% of the accuracy for 5% of the effort. If you want more precision, the Khamis-Roche method adds his current height, weight, and skeletal age. Pediatric endocrinologists use it. You can't run it at your kitchen table.

For a home estimate, mid-parental is what you want.

What NOT to do

  • Don't buy a "height increase" supplement. None of them work. All of them prey on anxious parents.
  • Don't project his height every month. Weekly measurement fluctuations are noise.
  • Don't compare him to his cousin or his classmate. His trajectory is his own.
  • Don't tell him you are trying to figure out how tall he will be. Have this conversation with your pediatrician, not with him.
