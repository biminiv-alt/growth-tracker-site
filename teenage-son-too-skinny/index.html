My Teenage Son Is Too Skinny. Should I Worry?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

A lot of teenage boys look all arms and legs for a while. Here is how to tell a healthy, lean kid from one who is underweight, and what to do if he wants to put on weight.

The short answer

Usually it is normal. Many boys shoot up in height before their weight and muscle catch up, so they look skinny for a year or two. If he has energy, eats regularly and is following his own line on the growth chart, being lean is not a problem.

Lean or underweight?

Healthy leanWorth checking
Weight steady or rising slowlyWeight falling
Following the same percentile line over timeDropping across percentile lines
Good energy and appetiteTired, low appetite, or stomach trouble
Thin parents or relativesNo family pattern of being thin

Doctors define underweight as a BMI below the 5th percentile for his age. His pediatrician can plot this for you.

If he wants to gain weight

  • Add one or two real snacks a day rather than forcing bigger meals.
  • Lean on calorie-dense foods: nuts and nut butters, whole milk, cheese, eggs, avocado, olive oil, granola.
  • Strength training with good coaching helps the weight go on as muscle. See does weightlifting stunt growth.
  • Be patient. Much of the filling out happens late in puberty, from about 15 to 18.

Shakes can help a small eater, but most boys do not need supplements. See does protein powder help boys grow.

When to talk to his pediatrician

  • He is losing weight or crossing percentile lines downward.
  • He has ongoing stomach pain, diarrhea or bloating.
  • He seems anxious about food or his body, skips meals, or exercises far more than his sport needs.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a teenage boy to be very skinny?

Often, yes. Many boys grow taller first and fill out later in puberty, so they look thin for a year or two. If he is growing, has energy and is following his own curve on the growth chart, being lean is usually healthy.

How do I know if my son is underweight?

Doctors use BMI-for-age. A BMI below the 5th percentile for his age is considered underweight. His pediatrician can plot it, along with his weight trend over time.

How can my skinny son gain weight healthily?

Add an extra snack or two a day, include calorie-dense foods like nuts, nut butters, whole milk, cheese, eggs and olive oil, and pair it with strength training if he is old enough.

When should I worry about my son being too thin?

Talk to his pediatrician if he is losing weight, dropping across percentile lines, often tired, has stomach problems, or seems worried about his body or food.
