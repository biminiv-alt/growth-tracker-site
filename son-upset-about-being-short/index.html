My Son Is Upset About Being Short. What Do I Say?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

He measured himself against his friends and came up short, and now he is asking you about it. Here is how to talk with him honestly without making it a bigger deal than it is.

Start by listening

When a boy says he hates being short, the height is often standing in for something else: being picked last, a nickname, a girl he likes, or just feeling behind his friends. Ask what happened before you jump to reassurance. "What made you think about it today?" gets you further than "You're fine."

What to say

  • "Lots of boys grow later than their friends, and they keep growing after their friends stop." This is true for most short-for-age boys, and it is the fact boys find most reassuring.
  • "Dad (or your uncle) was the same way." If someone in the family was a late bloomer, tell the story. It is powerful proof he can see.
  • "We can track it together." Measuring every few months turns worry into something he can watch change. Boys often like seeing the number go up.
  • "Your height doesn't decide what you're good at." Then name what he is good at, specifically.

What not to say

  • Promising a number. "You'll be six feet" sets him up for disappointment if he isn't. Predictions have a range of several inches. See how accurate height predictions are.
  • "It doesn't matter." It matters to him right now. Acknowledge that first.
  • Comparing him to siblings or classmates, even kindly.

Things he can actually control

Giving him a job helps. None of these will add inches beyond what his genes allow, but they help him reach his full potential and feel stronger in the meantime:

  • Sleep: 8 to 10 hours. Growth hormone is released mostly in deep sleep. See sleep and height in teen boys.
  • Food: enough calories and protein, especially if he is very active.
  • Strength and skill: sports, lifting with good form and practice build confidence regardless of height. Lifting does not stunt growth. See does weightlifting stunt growth.
  • Posture: standing straight genuinely adds a little visible height.

Steer him away from "grow taller" supplements and online programs. They do not work for healthy kids, and some contain things that can harm.

When it is more than a bad day

Talk with his pediatrician or school counselor if he is being teased or bullied, avoids school or activities he used to love, withdraws from friends, or seems sad or irritable most days. Those are signs he needs more support, whatever is happening with his height.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tell my son he will grow taller?

Avoid promising a number. You can honestly tell him that most boys who are shorter in middle school are on a later timeline and keep growing for years after their friends stop.

Do height supplements or stretching help?

No supplement, stretching routine or program has been shown to make a healthy boy taller. Some products contain hormones or stimulants that can do harm. Sleep, enough food and normal activity are what support growth.

When should I worry about how he feels about his height?

If he is avoiding school or activities he used to like, being teased, withdrawing, sleeping or eating very differently, or seems down most days, talk to his pediatrician or school counselor.

Should I take him to a doctor to reassure him?

A routine checkup where the pediatrician plots his growth can be reassuring for both of you. If his growth pace is normal, hearing that from the doctor often lands better than hearing it from mom.
