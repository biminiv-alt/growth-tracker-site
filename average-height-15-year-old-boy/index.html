What "average" means here

The average (50th percentile) height for a 15-year-old boy in the U.S. is 169.0 cm (5' 6.5"). That means about half of 15-year-old boys are taller than this, and half are shorter. It does not mean anything is wrong with a 15-year-old who is taller or shorter, every percentile from about the 3rd to the 97th falls within the range of typical variation.

Why boys the same age can be very different heights

At 15, most boys are well into puberty. Peak growth velocity typically happens in the 13-15 range, so many 15-year-olds are just past their fastest growth year. Others are still climbing.

By 15, the early-developers are winding down and the late-developers are just getting started. Both groups end up at similar adult heights on average, puberty timing shifts when the growth happens, not how much.

When to talk to a pediatrician

Height alone is rarely diagnostic. What pediatricians look for at age 15:

  • No signs of puberty by 15 warrants a pediatrician conversation.
  • Height below the 3rd percentile with no family explanation.
  • Sustained downward percentile crossing over 6-12 months.
  • Growth velocity below 1.5 inches per year during active puberty.

If any of these apply, that is a conversation to bring to his next visit, ideally with 6-12 months of measurements in hand.

Frequently asked questions

Is my 15-year-old short?

5' 2" (157 cm) at age 15 is around the 5-10th percentile. Shorter side, worth checking growth trend and family height context.

What if my 15-year-old is tall?

6' 0" (183 cm) at age 15 puts him above the 95th percentile, tall, and likely close to his final adult height.

Do boys still grow after 15?

Yes, most 15-year-old boys still have 2-3 inches of growth left. A minority grow into their early 20s.

How much should a 15-year-old grow in a year?

1-3 inches per year is typical at 15. Growth is starting to slow for early developers.
