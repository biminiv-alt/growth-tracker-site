My Son Is 13 and Hasn't Had a Growth Spurt Yet. Is That Normal?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Yes, in most cases. At 13 a big share of boys have not started their spurt, and many have not visibly started puberty. Here is how to tell normal-late from worth-a-look.

The short answer

Thirteen is right in the middle of the normal window. Boys start puberty anywhere from 9 to 14, and the fastest year of growth comes about 1.5 to 2 years after that start. Do the math and plenty of perfectly healthy boys will not see their spurt until 14, 15 or even 16.

That is why 13 is also the age with the widest height gap between classmates. A boy who started puberty at 10 can be half a foot taller than a boy who has not started yet, and both are on a normal path.

Signs the spurt is coming

  • His shoe size is climbing fast.
  • He is hungry all the time.
  • Body odor, oily skin or first pimples.
  • The first pubic or underarm hair.

Any of these at 13 means puberty is underway, and the height usually follows within a year or two. Our list of signs a growth spurt is coming covers the full sequence.

Where a 13-year-old usually stands

The average 13-year-old boy is about 5' 1.6" (156.4 cm), and the normal 5th to 95th percentile range runs from about 4' 8.5" to 5' 6.7" (143.6 to 169.5 cm), based on CDC growth data. See the full chart for 13-year-old boys.

A boy near the bottom of that range at 13 with no puberty signs yet is very often a classic late bloomer. The medical name is constitutional delay of growth and puberty. It runs in families and these boys usually catch up.

The one thing that matters most: his yearly pace

At 13, his height on one day tells you less than how fast he is growing. A boy growing a steady 2 inches or more a year is almost always fine, even if he is the shortest in his grade. A boy who grew under 2 inches in the past year and is sliding down the percentile lines is the one worth a conversation.

Pull his heights from his last two checkups, measure him now, and work out inches per year. Our guide to normal growth rates for boys shows what to compare against.

When to bring it up with the pediatrician

  • He grew less than 2 inches in the last 12 months.
  • He is below the 3rd percentile and nothing in the family explains it.
  • He has dropped across two percentile lines.
  • There are still no puberty signs by his 14th birthday.
  • He has other symptoms: tiredness, frequent stomach problems, headaches or a poor appetite.

Family history is your best clue

If his dad, an uncle or a grandfather was a late bloomer, a 13-year-old with no spurt is following a family script. Ask the men in the family when they shot up. It is one of the most useful things you can tell the pediatrician. More in if dad was a late bloomer.

What to do this month

  1. Measure him and write down the date.
  2. Find his last two recorded heights and work out his yearly pace.
  3. Note any puberty signs and roughly when they started.
  4. Ask the family about late growth spurts.
  5. If anything on the list above applies, book a visit and bring your notes.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a 13-year-old boy not to have a growth spurt?

Yes. The average boy's fastest growth comes around 13 to 14, which means many boys have not started it by 13. Late developers often peak at 15 or 16 and still reach a normal adult height.

When should a boy start puberty?

Anywhere from 9 to 14 is normal. Doctors usually take a closer look if there are no signs of puberty at all by 14.

How much should a 13-year-old boy grow in a year?

Before his spurt, about 2 to 2.5 inches a year. During the spurt, 3 to 4 inches. Less than 2 inches in a year is worth mentioning to his pediatrician.

Can I do anything to speed up his growth spurt?

No supplement or program speeds up a healthy boy's puberty. Good sleep, enough protein and calories, and normal activity support the growth his genes allow.
