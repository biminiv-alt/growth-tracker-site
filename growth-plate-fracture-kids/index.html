Growth Plate Fractures in Kids: Will It Affect His Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Hearing that a break involves the growth plate is scary. Most heal well. Here is what the growth plate is, why doctors keep checking it for months, and what a problem would look like.

The short answer

Most growth plate fractures heal without any effect on growth. Growth plates are the softer areas near the ends of a child's long bones where new bone is made. Because they are weaker than the bone around them, they are a common place for breaks in active kids. Estimates suggest roughly 15 to 30 percent of childhood fractures involve one.

Why some need closer watching

Doctors grade these breaks by how they cross the growth plate. Breaks that run along the plate usually heal well. Breaks that cross through it, or that crush it, carry a higher chance of the plate closing early or growing unevenly. Your son's doctor will tell you which kind he has.

  • Wrist, finger and ankle growth plate breaks are the most common, and most heal well.
  • Knee-area breaks are less common but are watched more closely because those plates add a lot of leg length.
  • The younger he is, the more growing that plate still has to do, so the follow-up matters more.

What follow-up looks like

  • Follow-up X-rays, often for 6 to 12 months, sometimes longer.
  • Checking that both arms or both legs stay the same length and straight.
  • Keeping to the return-to-sport plan. Going back too early is a common reason for re-injury.

Signs to call the doctor about later

  • One leg looking shorter, or a new limp
  • The arm or leg starting to look angled or bowed
  • Ongoing pain or stiffness months after it healed

Does it change his overall height?

Generally no. Even when a problem happens, it affects that one bone. His adult height is still mostly set by family genetics and puberty timing. See when growth plates close in boys and how sports affect boys' growth.

Frequently asked questions

Will a growth plate fracture stunt my son's growth?

Usually not. Most growth plate fractures heal without any lasting effect on growth. A small share can disturb growth in that one bone, which is why doctors follow them for months afterward.

Does a growth plate fracture affect overall height?

Generally no. A growth problem, if one happens, affects that single bone. A problem in a leg bone could cause one leg to end up shorter, but his overall height is not usually changed in a noticeable way.

How common are growth plate fractures?

Fairly common. Estimates suggest roughly 15 to 30 percent of childhood fractures involve a growth plate. They are more common in boys and during the fastest growth years.

How long do doctors follow a growth plate fracture?

Often for 6 to 12 months, and sometimes longer, with follow-up X-rays to make sure the bone is growing normally.
