When Do Boys Grow Facial Hair? Ages, Order and What It Says About Growth

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

The first fuzz on his upper lip is one of the later signs of puberty. Here is when it usually shows up, the order it fills in, and what it tells you about the growth he has left.

The short answer

Most boys get their first facial hair between about 13 and 16. It usually starts as light hair at the corners of the upper lip, a couple of years after puberty begins. A full beard can take until the late teens or twenties, and how thick it gets runs strongly in families.

The usual order

  1. Corners of the upper lip
  2. Across the whole upper lip
  3. Upper cheeks and sideburns
  4. The chin, then under the lip
  5. Lower cheeks and neck, often last

What it says about his height

Facial hair is a later sign, so by the time it appears, his fastest growth is usually happening or just behind him. Most boys still grow for another year or two after that, but more slowly. See how much boys grow after they start shaving.

When to ask his doctor

  • Facial hair before about age 10, especially with other puberty signs. See early puberty signs.
  • No signs of puberty at all by 14.

Frequently asked questions

At what age do boys start growing facial hair?

Most boys see their first facial hair between about 13 and 16, with a wide normal range. It usually comes a couple of years after puberty starts.

Where does facial hair grow first?

Usually at the corners of the upper lip, then across the upper lip, then the sideburns and chin. The cheeks and neck tend to fill in last.

Does facial hair mean he has stopped growing?

No. First facial hair usually arrives around or just after his fastest growth year, so most boys still have some height left. Growth slows over the following couple of years.

Is it normal for a 17-year-old to have little facial hair?

Yes. A full beard often does not fill in until the late teens or twenties, and how much a man grows depends a lot on family genes.
