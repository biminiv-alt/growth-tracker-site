What Does My Son's Height Percentile Mean?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

The pediatrician says he is in the 25th percentile. Is that good? Here is what height percentiles mean, what range is normal, and what to watch instead of a single number.

The short answer

A height percentile compares your son with other boys the same age. The 25th percentile means he is taller than about 25 out of 100 boys his age. The 50th is the middle. Most doctors treat roughly the 3rd to 97th percentile as the normal range, and they care more about how his percentile changes over time than about any single number.

Why the trend matters more

  • Staying on his line year after year, even a low one, is a healthy pattern. See when his percentile stays the same.
  • Drifting down across lines over a year or more is what doctors look into. See what to track if his growth slows.

Frequently asked questions

What does the 50th percentile for height mean?

It means he is taller than about half of boys his age and shorter than about half. It is the middle, or average.

Is the 25th percentile for height bad?

No. The 25th percentile means he is taller than about 25 out of 100 boys his age. Anything from roughly the 3rd to the 97th percentile is within the normal range.

What matters more than his percentile?

His trend over time. A boy who stays near the same percentile line year after year is growing normally, even on a low line. Moving down across lines is what doctors watch for.

Does his percentile predict his adult height?

Only roughly, and less well during puberty. Boys who start puberty late often drop in percentile at 11 to 14 and then catch up. Family height is a better guide.
