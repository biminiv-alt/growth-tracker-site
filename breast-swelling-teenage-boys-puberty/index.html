Breast Swelling in Teenage Boys: Is It Normal?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

A sore, rubbery lump under the nipple can scare a boy and his parents. In puberty it is common and usually temporary. Here is what it is, how long it lasts, and the few signs that need a doctor.

The short answer

It is common and usually goes away on its own. Doctors call it pubertal gynecomastia. During puberty, hormone levels shift, and for a while the balance can tip enough to grow a small disc of breast tissue under one or both nipples. Estimates vary, but somewhere between a third and more than half of boys notice it, most often around ages 12 to 14.

What it usually looks and feels like

  • A firm or rubbery lump, about the size of a coin, right under the nipple
  • Tender or sore, especially when bumped or when a shirt rubs
  • On one side or both, and often bigger on one side
  • Shows up during puberty, not before it

Soft fullness spread across the chest with no distinct lump is often just fat, sometimes called pseudogynecomastia. That is a different thing and tends to follow overall weight.

How long it lasts

Most cases settle within 6 months to 2 years as hormone levels even out later in puberty. The large majority are gone by the late teens. Treatment is rarely needed.

When to see his pediatrician

  • It started before any other signs of puberty. See early puberty signs in boys.
  • It is growing quickly, is very large, or is hard and does not move.
  • There is nipple discharge, skin dimpling, or a lump elsewhere, including in a testicle.
  • It is still there after about 2 years, or past age 17.
  • He is using muscle-building supplements, anabolic steroids, marijuana, or medicines that can cause it. Bring a list of everything he takes.

Helping him with it

  • Tell him plainly that it is common and usually temporary. Many boys assume something is badly wrong.
  • Looser shirts or a fitted athletic undershirt can help with tenderness and self-consciousness.
  • If it is affecting sports, swimming or how he feels, say so at his checkup. Doctors take that seriously.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a boy's chest to swell during puberty?

Yes. Doctors call it pubertal gynecomastia. Estimates vary, but somewhere between a third and more than half of boys get some breast tissue swelling during puberty, most often around ages 12 to 14.

How long does puberty breast swelling last?

Most cases settle on their own within 6 months to 2 years, and the large majority are gone by the late teens.

Can it be on just one side?

Yes. It often starts on one side, or is bigger on one side, and can be tender to touch.

When should a doctor look at it?

See his pediatrician if it starts before puberty, grows quickly, is hard or fixed in place, has nipple discharge or skin changes, is still there after about 2 years or past age 17, or if he is taking supplements, steroids or certain medicines.
