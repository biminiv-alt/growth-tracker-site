When Do Growth Plates Close in Boys?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Once a boy's growth plates close, his height is set. Here is when that usually happens, the signs it is getting close, and how doctors can tell for sure.

The short answer

In most boys the growth plates close between about 15 and 18. Boys who started puberty late tend to be on the later end, which is why late bloomers can keep growing after their friends have stopped.

Age alone does not tell you much. How far along he is in puberty tells you far more.

What growth plates are

Growth plates are areas of soft, growing cartilage near the ends of the long bones. During childhood they add new bone, which makes the bones longer. Late in puberty, rising sex hormones cause the plates to harden and fuse. After that, the bones can no longer get longer.

Signs his growth plates are closing

  • His growth has slowed right down. Less than about half an inch over a full year, measured carefully, usually means he is nearly done.
  • He is well past his growth spurt. The fastest growth was a year or more ago.
  • He is shaving regularly and has an adult voice and body hair. See how much boys grow after they start shaving.
  • His shoe size has stopped changing. Feet stop growing before height does.

Signs like these point to the end of growth but cannot give a number. Measure him the same way every few months: see how to measure accurately at home.

How doctors can tell for sure

A bone age x-ray of the left hand and wrist shows how mature his skeleton is. If his bone age is younger than his real age, he likely has more growing time left than his friends. If his growth plates are already nearly closed, there is little height left. It is a quick, low-dose x-ray, and it is the most useful single test for this question.

When to talk to the doctor

Ask his pediatrician about a bone age x-ray if he is 14 or older with no signs of puberty, if his growth has slowed while he is still short for his family, or if you simply want a clearer picture of how much growth is left. See when to ask for a pediatric endocrinology referral.

Frequently asked questions

At what age do boys' growth plates close?

In most boys the growth plates in the legs and spine close between about 15 and 18. Late bloomers can keep open growth plates and keep growing a bit longer.

Can a boy grow after his growth plates close?

No. Once the growth plates have fused, the long bones cannot get longer, so height does not increase. Small changes in measured height can still come from posture or time of day.

How do you know if growth plates are closed?

A bone age x-ray of the left hand and wrist shows how mature his bones are and whether growth plates are still open. Your pediatrician or a pediatric endocrinologist can order it.

Can anything keep growth plates open longer?

There is no safe, proven way for a healthy boy to delay growth plate closure at home. Treatments that affect puberty timing are only used by specialists for specific medical conditions.
