Knee Pain During a Growth Spurt: Osgood-Schlatter in Boys

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

If your active son has a painful bump just below his kneecap, especially during a growth spurt, the likely cause is Osgood-Schlatter disease. It sounds alarming but is common and temporary.

The short answer

A painful bump just below the kneecap in an active boy aged about 12 to 15 is most often Osgood-Schlatter disease. It is irritation of a growth plate where the kneecap tendon attaches to the shinbone. It is common, it goes away as he finishes growing, and it does not affect his height.

Signs

  • Pain and tenderness at a bony bump just below the kneecap
  • Pain that gets worse with running, jumping, kneeling or stairs, and eases with rest
  • Swelling at the bump, in one knee or both

Why growth spurts trigger it

During a spurt, bones grow faster than the muscles and tendons around them. The tight thigh muscles pull hard on the growth plate below the knee with every jump and sprint, and it gets sore. See peak growth velocity in boys.

What helps

  • Cutting back on the activities that hurt most, without stopping all activity
  • Ice on the bump for 15 to 20 minutes after sports
  • Gentle stretching of the front and back of the thighs
  • Knee pads for sports with kneeling or contact
  • Pain relief medicine if his pediatrician says it is OK

Symptoms often come and go for 12 to 24 months. A painless bump may stay for good.

When to see a doctor

  • Pain that is not at the bump, or pain in the joint itself
  • Pain at night or at rest, a limp, redness, warmth or fever
  • Pain after a fall or a sudden injury
  • Pain that keeps him out of normal activity for weeks

Frequently asked questions

What is Osgood-Schlatter disease?

It is irritation where the kneecap tendon attaches to a growth plate at the top of the shinbone. Repeated pulling from running and jumping during a growth spurt makes the spot sore and sometimes swollen.

At what age do boys get Osgood-Schlatter?

Most often between about 12 and 15 in boys, during or around the growth spurt, and mostly in kids who play running and jumping sports.

How long does Osgood-Schlatter last?

Symptoms often come and go for 12 to 24 months and usually stop once the growth plate matures. A painless bump below the knee may remain.

Does Osgood-Schlatter affect height?

No. It does not stunt growth or change adult height.

Can he keep playing sports with Osgood-Schlatter?

Many kids keep playing with adjustments, cutting back when pain is bad. His pediatrician or a sports medicine doctor can guide how much is OK.
