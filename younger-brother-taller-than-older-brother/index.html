Younger Brother Taller Than His Older Brother? What It Means

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

It stings when a little brother passes a big brother. Here is why it happens so often, and what it does and does not tell you about how tall your older son will be.

The short answer

It is common and usually normal. Brothers with the same parents often end up several inches apart. A younger brother can pass an older one because he started puberty earlier, because he inherited a taller mix of genes, or both.

Why brothers end up different heights

  • Different gene mix. Each child inherits a different combination of height genes from both parents. That is why the family-height formula gives a range of about 3 to 4 inches either way, not one number. See how accurate mid-parental height is.
  • Different puberty timing. One brother may start at 11 and the other at 14. The early one shoots up first and can look much taller for a few years.
  • Health and food. A long illness, a medical condition or very poor eating during the growth years can hold one child back.

Handling it at home

For the older brother, being passed can hurt. Avoid comparing them out loud, and keep the focus on what each is good at. See when your son is upset about being short.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a younger brother to be taller than his older brother?

Yes. Brothers can differ by several inches as adults, and a younger brother passing an older one, for a while or for good, is common.

Will my older son catch up to his younger brother?

Often, if the older son is simply a later bloomer. If both are done growing, the gap usually comes from genes, which shuffle differently for each child.

Why are brothers different heights?

Each child gets a different mix of height genes from both parents, and boys go through puberty at different ages. Health and nutrition can also play a part.

When should I check with a doctor?

If either son is falling across percentile lines on his growth chart, growing less than about 2 inches a year before puberty, or shows no signs of puberty by 14, talk to his pediatrician.
