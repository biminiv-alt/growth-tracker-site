Does Protein Powder Help Boys Grow Taller?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Protein shakes are a popular fix for skinny or short teen boys. Here is how much protein he actually needs, and whether extra protein can make a difference to his height.

The short answer

Protein powder does not make a well-fed boy taller. Protein is essential for growth, but once a boy gets enough, extra protein does not add height. Most boys in the US already get more than the recommended amount from food.

For a sense of scale: a glass of milk has about 8 grams, an egg about 6, a cup of Greek yogurt about 20, and a chicken breast about 25 to 30.

When a shake can help

  • He is a very picky eater. See picky eaters and growth.
  • He has a small appetite or is an athlete who cannot eat enough to keep up.
  • He is losing weight or dropping across percentile lines. That one needs his pediatrician, not just a shake.

A shake made with milk adds calories, protein and calcium at once.

What to watch for

  • Loose regulation. Supplements are not approved by the FDA before they are sold, and some have been found with heavy metals or ingredients not on the label. Look for third-party tested products.
  • Added extras. Avoid powders with caffeine, "test boosters" or other performance claims.
  • Replacing meals. Shakes work best on top of meals, not instead of them.

Frequently asked questions

Will protein powder make my son taller?

Not if he already eats enough protein, and most boys in the US do. Extra protein beyond what the body needs does not add height.

How much protein does a teenage boy need?

About 34 grams a day for ages 9 to 13 and about 52 grams a day for ages 14 to 18, more for very active athletes. A glass of milk has about 8 grams and a chicken breast about 25 to 30.

Is protein powder safe for teenagers?

Most food-based powders are not harmful in normal amounts, but supplements are loosely regulated and some have been found with contaminants. Real food is the first choice.

When can a protein shake help?

For a very picky eater, a boy with a small appetite, or an athlete who struggles to eat enough, a shake made with milk can help fill the gap. Check with his pediatrician if he is losing weight or falling off his growth curve.
