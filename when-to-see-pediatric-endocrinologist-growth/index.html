When Should a Parent Ask for a Pediatric Endocrinology Referral?

Most short boys never need an endocrinologist. But there are specific patterns where a referral genuinely helps. Here is when to ask, and what happens at the visit.

The short answer

Pediatric endocrinology referrals for growth make sense in a narrow set of situations. Most short boys, including most late bloomers, do not need one. The point of the referral is to rule out the small number of treatable causes of short stature, and to weigh treatment options in confirmed cases.

Specific patterns that warrant a referral

  • Height below the 3rd percentile AND family history does not explain it.
  • Percentile crossing downward more than one major band over 6-18 months.
  • Growth velocity below age ranges. Under 2 in/yr age 6-12 pre-puberty, under 1.5 in/yr during the spurt.
  • No signs of puberty by 14 AND slow growth AND no family history of late bloomers.
  • Very slow bone age (more than 2 years behind) with slow growth.
  • Suspicion of a specific cause: chronic illness, hypothyroid symptoms, syndromic features, prior head trauma.

Patterns that usually do NOT warrant referral

  • Short but tracking. Low percentile his whole life, normal velocity, average family.
  • Late puberty with clear family history.
  • Short parents predicting a lower-percentile boy who tracks to that.
  • One slow year during recovery from illness.

What happens at the visit

  1. Full growth history plotted on a chart.
  2. Physical exam with Tanner staging.
  3. Bone age x-ray.
  4. Blood work: TSH, IGF-1, basic metabolic panel, celiac screening, LH/FSH/testosterone for pubertal boys.
  5. Growth hormone stimulation testing if warranted.
  6. Pituitary MRI if there is concern about a structural cause.

What GH therapy actually treats

Approved for documented GH deficiency, Turner syndrome, chronic kidney disease affecting growth, Prader-Willi, idiopathic short stature (height > 2.25 SD below mean), SGA without catch-up.

Not approved for constitutional delay in an otherwise healthy boy. Late bloomers do not get GH.

How to prepare

  1. Height records going back as far as possible.
  2. Family height for both parents and grandparents.
  3. Family history of late puberty on both sides.
  4. Your specific concerns written down.
  5. His own view.
