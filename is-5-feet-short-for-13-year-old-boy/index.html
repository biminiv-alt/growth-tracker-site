Is 5 Feet Short for a 13-Year-Old Boy?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Five feet is one of the most common heights parents ask about at 13. Here is where it falls on the CDC growth charts, and what the number does and does not tell you.

The short answer

No. A 13-year-old boy who is 5'0" is around the 30th percentile on CDC growth charts. That means about 7 in 10 boys his age are taller and 3 in 10 are shorter. It is a normal height, a little under the average of about 5'1.5" at 13.

What matters more than one number

  • His growth speed. A boy at the 30th percentile who keeps growing is on track. See normal growth speed for boys.
  • Where he is in puberty. A boy who has not started his spurt yet will often pass many classmates later.
  • Your family's heights. Shorter parents usually mean a shorter, healthy son.

When to ask his doctor

Below the 3rd percentile, dropping across two percentile lines, or growing less than about 2 inches a year before puberty is worth a visit.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5 feet short for a 13-year-old boy?

No. On CDC charts a 13-year-old boy at 5'0" is around the 30th percentile, which is within the normal range.

What is the average height of a 13-year-old boy?

About 5'1.5" (156 cm) at his 13th birthday on CDC charts, rising to about 5'4.5" by 14.

Is 5 feet short for a 14-year-old boy?

It is on the shorter side. At 14, 5'0" is around the 8th percentile, still in the normal range but worth tracking his growth speed.

Will a 5-foot 13-year-old grow much more?

Usually yes. Many boys have their fastest growth year between 13 and 15, often 3 to 4 inches in that year.
