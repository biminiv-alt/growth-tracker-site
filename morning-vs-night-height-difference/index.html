Why Is My Son Taller in the Morning?

Your son really is about half an inch taller in the morning than at night. It is not a measurement error. Here is why, and how to control for it in a growth log.

The short answer

Yes, he is taller in the morning. The difference is usually 0.5 to 1 inch (1.25 to 2.5 cm). It resets each night while he sleeps.

Why it happens

The spine has 23 intervertebral discs filled with a gel-like fluid. During the day, gravity and body weight slowly compress those discs, and some of the fluid is squeezed out. The spine literally shortens. At night, lying down releases the pressure, the discs rehydrate, and the spine returns to its full length.

  • Standing all day, doing sports, or carrying a backpack all increase compression.
  • Sleep restores the length. Most of the recovery happens in the first 2-3 hours.
  • Astronauts in zero gravity gain 1-2 inches temporarily because there is no gravitational compression at all.

How to control for it in your log

  • Measure at the same time of day every time. Morning is most reliable.
  • If you can only measure in the evening, subtract about 0.5 inch to compare to morning numbers.
  • Keep the position identical: bare feet, back and heels against a wall, head level.
  • Note the time on your log so you can compare apples to apples.

When it matters most

The morning-versus-night difference matters most during periods of slow growth. If he grew 0.5 inch in six months, an evening measurement could hide that entire gain. During a fast growth spurt, when he grows 1-2 inches in six months, it matters less but still enough to be worth being consistent.

What it does NOT tell you

The morning-versus-night difference is not a sign of anything unusual. It happens to everyone, adults included. Adults gain about 0.5 to 0.75 inch overnight too.
