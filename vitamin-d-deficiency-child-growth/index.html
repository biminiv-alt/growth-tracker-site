Can Vitamin D Deficiency Affect a Child's Growth?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed September 2026

Vitamin D deficiency can slow growth and impair bone development. Correcting a deficiency restores normal growth. Supplementing beyond deficiency does not increase height.

The short answer

Yes, vitamin D deficiency can affect growth. Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption and bone development. Severe deficiency causes rickets in children, which can permanently affect bone growth. Mild deficiency is more common and can slow growth in some kids. Correcting a deficiency restores normal growth. Beyond that, extra vitamin D does not make a well-nourished child taller.

How vitamin D affects growth

  • Calcium absorption. Without vitamin D, kids cannot absorb dietary calcium efficiently. Bones do not mineralize properly.
  • Growth plate function. Vitamin D receptors in growth plates influence cell development. Deficiency impairs growth plate maturation.
  • Muscle strength. Vitamin D affects muscle function. Deficiency contributes to weakness and delayed motor development.
  • Immune function. Chronic illness from immune dysfunction can secondarily slow growth.

Signs of deficiency

  • Slowed growth velocity below age-appropriate range.
  • Bone pain, especially in legs.
  • Muscle weakness or delayed motor milestones.
  • Bowed legs (in more severe or younger cases).
  • Delayed tooth eruption.
  • Frequent infections.

Who is at higher risk

  • Kids with darker skin (melanin reduces vitamin D synthesis from sun).
  • Kids in northern latitudes with limited sun exposure.
  • Kids who avoid dairy and are not on a fortified alternative.
  • Exclusively breastfed infants not receiving vitamin D drops.
  • Kids with celiac, inflammatory bowel disease, or other malabsorption conditions.
  • Kids on certain medications (some anti-seizure drugs).
  • Kids with obesity (vitamin D is fat-soluble and sequestered).

How deficiency is diagnosed

A single blood test: 25-hydroxyvitamin D level. Normal is generally considered 30 ng/mL and above. Deficiency is below 20 ng/mL. Between 20 and 30 is insufficient in most guidelines. Cost is low; most primary care doctors will order it if concern exists.

How it is treated

Vitamin D3 supplementation at a dose based on the deficiency severity and the child's weight. Typical maintenance is 600-1000 IU per day for children over 1. Higher therapeutic doses (2000-5000 IU/day) are used to correct deficiency, then reduced. Recheck level after 8-12 weeks.

Does extra vitamin D increase height?

No, not in kids who are already sufficient. Supplementing beyond correcting a deficiency has not been shown to increase adult height. There is a floor effect: enough is enough. More is not more.

When to ask about testing

  • Slow growth velocity plus any risk factors.
  • Bone pain in a growing child.
  • Delayed motor milestones.
  • Family history of vitamin D deficiency or rickets.
  • Kids over 1 who never took a vitamin D supplement and rarely consume dairy or sun exposure.
