Why Is My Teenage Son Sleeping So Much?

By The Growth Path Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Sleeping until noon on Saturday is a teenage classic. Here is why it happens, how it connects to growth, and the signs that point to something more than a normal teen.

The short answer

It is usually normal. Teens need 8 to 10 hours of sleep a night. During puberty their body clock shifts later, so they cannot fall asleep early, then school starts early and they run short all week. Long weekend sleep-ins are mostly catch-up.

How growth fits in

Growth hormone is released mostly during deep sleep, and building bone and muscle takes energy. Many parents notice their son sleeping more around a growth spurt. Sleep will not make him taller than his genes allow, but good sleep supports normal growth. More in sleep and height in teenage boys.

Helping him get enough on school nights

  • Phones and games out of the bedroom an hour before lights out.
  • A steady wake time, even on weekends, within an hour or two of school days.
  • No caffeine after lunch. See does caffeine stunt growth.
  • Morning daylight helps reset a late body clock.

When tiredness needs a doctor

  • He is exhausted even after enough sleep.
  • He snores loudly, gasps or stops breathing in his sleep.
  • He seems down, irritable, or has lost interest in things he enjoyed.
  • He has a lasting sore throat, fevers, pale skin, or weight loss.

These can point to sleep apnea, low iron, mono, thyroid problems, or low mood, all of which his pediatrician can check.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for a teenage boy to sleep a lot?

Yes. Teens need 8 to 10 hours of sleep a night, and puberty shifts their body clock later. Most get too little on school nights and catch up on weekends.

Do teenagers sleep more during growth spurts?

Many parents notice their son sleeping more during a growth spurt. Growth hormone is released mostly in deep sleep, and growing takes energy, but the main driver of long weekend sleep is usually catching up on lost sleep.

How much sleep does a teenage boy need?

Sleep experts recommend 8 to 10 hours a night for teens aged 13 to 18.

When is too much sleep a warning sign?

Talk to his pediatrician if he is tired even after enough sleep, snores loudly, has lost interest in things he used to enjoy, seems down, or has other symptoms like weight loss, fevers or a sore throat that will not go away.
